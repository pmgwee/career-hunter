## Tailoring Rules (learned through ~10 real applications — do not relearn these)

### Summary line
- **Never** write "targeting X roles" unless the role title matches the posting exactly.
- Hard ceiling of **3 lines**. Two is better.
- Lead with what the employer weights most: academics for graduate schemes, shipped production
  systems for engineering roles.
- Name concrete context over generic phrasing — *"a major Malaysian bank's platform"* beats
  *"business-critical software."*

### Section order by role type

| Role type | Order |
|---|---|
| Consulting (EY, Accenture) | Summary → Education → Skills → Experience → **Achievements** → Projects → Volunteering |
| Enterprise IT / energy (ExxonMobil, PETRONAS) | Summary → Education → Skills → Experience → Projects → Achievements → Volunteering |
| AI / engineering (Deriv, Grab, MoneyLion) | Summary → Skills → **Projects** → Experience → Education → Achievements |

Rule of thumb: **Experience above Projects** for consulting and enterprise;
**Projects above Experience** wherever the posting says any version of *"show me what you've built."*

Within Experience, use **relevance order**, not strict reverse-chronological:
iFAST → Ober Mountain → Yierming Production. (For AI/engineering roles, cut Ober Mountain.)

### Skills line
- Reorder so the **employer's own vocabulary appears first**.
- Delivery-methodology terms (Agile Sprints, Requirements Specification, UAT, Change Request
  Management, CI/CD) are all evidenced in the iFAST bullets — surface them for enterprise and
  consulting roles.
- Drop SEO and Tailwind CSS for anything enterprise or technical.

### Projects

- **LLM-Backed Music Recommendation is the weakest entry — cut it first when space is tight.**
- Promote **Blockchain For Good** into the top three achievements for any role naming

### Volunteering
Keep all three for national, community-facing or sustainability-positioned employers
(PETRONAS, EY, ExxonMobil). **Cut first for pure engineering roles.**

## Cover Letter Structure (four paragraphs)

Personal letterhead → recipient block with right-aligned date → `Dear Sir / Madam,` (or the
person's name for a direct approach) → bold underlined subject line in caps → four paragraphs
(first unnumbered, then `2.` `3.` `4.`) → "Thank you in advance for your kind consideration."
→ "Sincerely," → signature block.

1. **Role + credentials + availability.** Role applied for, academics (CGPA, Dean's List, MBA),
   Work & Travel as a single clause, availability. **Must also name something specific and
   researched about the employer — this is the paragraph that has been weakest.**
2. **iFAST.** Spec translation for the bank, compliance job, SQL optimisation — then the
   *lesson*: regulated data carries handling rules before business value; business requirements
   come before technical specification.
3. **Projects and self-directed work**, reframed for this specific employer. Name a real
   failure and what it taught. This is the strongest paragraph in the archive — keep the pattern.
4. **Ober Mountain, languages, competitions.** Add volunteering for national or
   community-facing employers. Close with a contact line.

### Voice rules (learned through iteration)
- Signpost each paragraph ("The reason I believe I would fit here starts with…").
- Name gaps honestly, then reframe. Reads as maturity, not weakness.
- No hedging openers ("I believe I would bring something useful").
- No generic pledges ("guarantee 100% effort") — they carry no information.
- Any emotional appeal goes **last**, after the evidence, and appears **once only**.
- Keep contractions consistent; don't mix "I have" and "I've".
- **Target 350–420 words of body** (header and credentials not counted). This is the
  career-ops house rule from `modes/cover.md` Step 6, not a preference — the cover mode
  reports whether the draft hit it. The Deriv letter ran ~700 and paragraph 4 lost the reader.

### Recurring grammar failure modes — check every draft
- Comma splices where a colon belongs.
- Full stop where a colon belongs (*"end to end. designing the system architecture"*).
- Gerund openers on resume bullets ("Earning…" should be "Earned…").
- Missing spaces after full stops and before brackets.
- Dangling possessives.
- British vs American spelling mixed across CV and letter (`optimised` vs `optimizing`) — pick
  **American** and hold it, since most tech employers here use US spelling.
